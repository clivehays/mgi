Every one of those was a small decision that something was not worth saying. None of them felt like a decision at the time. They do not arrive as a complaint later either, because the moment passed and the work carried on, which is how a team gets quieter without anyone choosing it.

The good news, and it is real

This one costs you the first awkward thirty seconds and nothing after that.

No tool, no process, no meeting added to anyone's week. Alignment turns on asking for the disagreement and then waiting long enough to get it. The waiting is the difficult part, and it is also the whole method.

  • Bad news reaches you while it is still small.
  • Somebody pushes back in the room instead of afterwards.
  • You hear about the problem from the person holding it.
  • You stop being the last to know.

Get the other half

Clive Hays built the Manager Gap Index.

Say your own current plan out loud and ask what is wrong with it. Then say nothing until somebody answers. The length of that silence is the reading, and it is worth timing.

Each ring is one condition of a working team, read from three things you were asked about. A solid blue segment means that one reached you inside the month. An amber segment means it last reached you over a month ago. A dashed amber segment means you could not recall it at all. The five are Readiness, Results, Involvement, Direction and Alignment, and they are always in the same order so the shape becomes familiar.

Output is holding, which is what makes this one awkward. Nothing in the numbers is asking you a question, while the behaviours that produce those numbers have stopped reaching you. Drift is the state managers miss most, because everything that would reveal it is something that quietly stops happening.
