Eight questions, about ninety seconds. Your team’s state appears on this page the moment you finish, before anything is asked of you.

For 45 of the first 51 managers, these eight questions named the same state as the full twenty.

  • Cruise
  • Drift
  • Headwinds
  • Stall
The four states
  1. Cruise Output holding, and the behaviours of a healthy team still reach you.
  2. Drift Output holding while the conditions underneath it slide. Nothing in the numbers says anything is wrong.
  3. Headwinds Output under pressure, with a real force outside the team acting on it.
  4. Stall Output has stopped moving, and the signals went quiet long before it did.
What you get

Straight away, with nothing asked of you:

  • The state your team is most likely in, and what that state actually means.
  • Your instinct going in, set against what your own answers support.

Then, if you want it sent to you:

  • A signal score out of nine, with each question and how far back your answer reached.
  • How much of your team’s week you are positioned to see.
  • One move for this week, chosen for the question that has gone longest.
  • The worksheet from the methodology library that runs that move.
Before you start

Answer from memory of actual events. There are no wrong answers. But a guessed answer makes the result meaningless. “Can’t recall” is a valid and useful answer.

How this compares with the full assessment

How this compares with the full assessment

The full assessment asks twenty questions. This one keeps the instinct check it opens with, three of its fifteen evidence questions, and the four that place your team.

For 45 of the first 51 managers who answered all twenty, these eight named the same state. Where the two differed, the short version pointed at the harder reading every time, never the easier one.

What it does not do: name which of the five areas your attention belongs in, or how far your picture and your team’s reality are likely to sit apart. Both of those need all fifteen questions, which is what the full assessment is for.

For anyone who leads a team directly: managers, heads of department, directors, and founders with people reporting to them. Company size does not matter. It asks about one team, the one you lead now.

No name and no company. The state is yours whether or not you give one. An email address is only for the full scorecard and the worksheet, and nothing individual is ever published.
